Something I did not expect to learn from a CIFAR-10 classifier.

For my Track 2 project at LearnDepth Academy I was asked a question that sounds
simple: can an ML system recognise when it should not trust its own prediction?

The textbook answer is yes. Calibrate the confidence, add abstention, add OOD
detection. So I implemented all of it - temperature scaling, MC Dropout, a
4-member deep ensemble, Mahalanobis feature distance. Seven uncertainty signals,
all evaluated on identical held-out data.

On clean data it worked beautifully. 72% accuracy, ECE of 0.04. Well calibrated.

Then I added noise.

The same checkpoint's confidence climbed to 0.84 while its accuracy fell to 16%.
And when I finally built the measurement to understand why, the standard
confidence signal scored 0.31 AUROC at detecting those inputs.

Worse than a coin flip. Actively pointing the wrong way.

The inputs it missed were caught almost perfectly - 0.92 - by a simple
feature-familiarity score instead.

That was the real lesson, and it was not the one I was expecting. Confidence and
familiarity are different quantities. Post-hoc calibration re-scales a number; it
cannot manufacture familiarity with an input the model has never seen. No
rescaling can.

So the final system fuses several signals into an explicit TRUST / REVIEW /
ABSTAIN verdict, with thresholds fit on held-out calibration data at a chosen
coverage target rather than hand-tuned.

Built with PyTorch, scikit-learn for the Ledoit-Wolf covariance, and Streamlit +
Plotly for an interactive dashboard. Every evaluation metric hand-implemented
so the definitions were auditable rather than library defaults. Zero cloud
credits - it runs on a laptop.

The habit I took away: do not treat the assigned problem as a spec. The credit
for building the expected thing is much lower than the credit for noticing that
the expected thing is wrong.

Repo and full write-up: https://github.com/madiredypalvasha-06/ML-T2-016-TrustGuard

1. Go to LinkedIn -> "Start a post" (or the top feed, then the box).
2. Paste the text above.
3. BEFORE you click Publish, check the visibility dropdown.

   *** THIS IS THE STEP THAT MATTERS ***

   It must say "Anyone" or "Public".

   Do NOT leave it on "Connections only". Your evaluation team will not be in
   your connections, so a connections-only post is a dead link for them.

4. Publish.
5. Open the published post in a fresh INCOGNITO window and confirm it loads with
   no sign-in prompt. If LinkedIn shows a login wall, go back and fix the
   visibility setting, then re-test.
6. Copy the URL from the address bar. It looks like:

   https://www.linkedin.com/posts/<your-name>_trustguard-<id>

   That is what you paste into form field 21.
